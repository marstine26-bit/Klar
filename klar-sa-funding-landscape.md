# Klar — South African Seed/Early-Stage Funding Landscape
*Research brief prepared August 2026. Builds on klar-pitch-materials.md (UK/Seedrs raise) and klar-competitive-brief.md. This document covers South African funding vehicles only — it is a separate, parallel track to the UK Seedrs campaign, not a replacement for it.*

---

## 0. Headline findings — read this first

1. **Section 12J is dead and has no successor.** 12J (the tax-incentive vehicle that made SA venture capital companies attractive to local retail investors) sunset for new investments years ago, and by end of June 2026 every existing 12J investment reaches its mandatory five-year maturity with investors exiting. **No replacement incentive has been introduced.** Some SA VC firms (e.g. Kalon Venture Partners) still carry "12J" in their branding/URL from their historical fund structure — this reflects legacy fund vehicles, not a currently-available tax incentive for new capital. **There is no SA equivalent of SEIS/EIS to offer investors right now.** This is the single biggest structural difference from the UK raise and should be stated plainly in any SA-facing pitch rather than implied.
2. **Ownership/B-BBEE is a hard eligibility gate for several vehicles, not just a scoring factor.** The National Empowerment Fund (NEF) requires a minimum of 51% black South African ownership to qualify at all. AlphaCode Incubate (the fintech-specific vehicle with the strongest sector fit) requires the applicant business be at least 51% owned *and managed* by black South Africans. SEFA requires 100% South African ownership. These are not "nice to have" scorecard points — they are pass/fail eligibility criteria. **[FOUNDER INPUT NEEDED: Klar's current cap table and ownership structure are not known to this research. Whether Klar qualifies for any B-BBEE-gated vehicle depends entirely on who owns the company today and is not something to assume or estimate.]**
3. **Klar's materials are currently UK-entity-centric.** The existing pitch describes a Seedrs raise, SEIS/EIS, and FCA AISP registration — all UK regulatory/legal concepts. Several SA vehicles (SEFA, NEF, TIA Seed Fund) require the *applicant entity itself* to be South African-registered (CIPC) with specific SA ownership percentages — not merely "has SA users" or "has an SA subsidiary with minority local ownership." Generalist SA/pan-African VC funds (Knife Capital, 4Di Capital, HAVAÍC) are more flexible and commonly invest into UK or Mauritius holding companies with SA operating subsidiaries, but this must be confirmed per-fund, not assumed. **[FOUNDER INPUT NEEDED: current legal entity status — is there an SA-registered entity (Pty Ltd) at all today, or does Klar operate in SA purely as a UK company serving SA users? This determines which vehicles are even reachable.]**

## 1. Shortlist — genuinely live vehicles worth pursuing

### Tier 1: Best strategic fit, worth prioritising

**HAVAÍC** (Cape Town)
- Status: Active. African Innovation Fund 3 ($50M target) reached third close (~$30M+ committed) as of February 2026, on track for final close in August 2026. Fund 3 has deployed ~$10M across 8 companies to date.
- Focus: Early-stage, high-growth, **post-revenue** African tech businesses with regional/global potential. FinTech is an explicit focus sector (recent deals include SAPay and Entersekt).
- Check size: Not fixed-disclosed but fund-level activity suggests meaningful early-stage checks (fund average implied ~$1–3M per portfolio company across prior funds).
- Requires: Post-revenue traction (Klar is pre-revenue per existing materials — **this may be a timing gate, not just a fit question**; worth confirming directly whether pre-revenue-with-live-product qualifies for a conversation even if not immediate investment).
- Application format: Direct outreach/pitch deck; no public online application form identified — relationship-led.
- Source: [HAVAÍC Fund 3 third close — Disrupt Africa](https://disruptafrica.com/2026/02/18/sas-havaic-announces-3rd-close-of-50m-3rd-fund-makes-4-investments/), [Launch Base Africa](https://launchbaseafrica.com/2026/02/16/cape-towns-havaic-hits-30m-milestone-for-third-fund-as-institutional-interest-hardens/)

**4Di Capital** (Cape Town)
- Status: Active. ~$60M AUM, backed 30–50 companies across 15+ African countries; strong fintech track record given Africa's mobile-first financial services adoption.
- Focus: Pre-seed, seed, and Series A. Fintech and crypto/fintech bridging noted as a focus area.
- Check size: Initial checks reported in the $250K–$2M range (one source narrows this to $250K–$1.5M).
- Application format: Pitch deck + direct approach; standard VC process (deck, data room, meetings) rather than a formal application portal.
- Source: [4Di Capital — VC Beast](https://vcbeast.com/vc-firms/4di-capital), [Tracxn](https://tracxn.com/d/venture-capital/4di-capital/__UQsXMkDvvb1tESTJ1bSCQn_zY0c1Xp1n1kbudwOKDzg)

**Knife Capital** (Cape Town)
- Status: Active, one of SA's most established VCs. Manages Knife Fund I, II (KNF Ventures) and Fund III, ~$50M total AUM.
- Focus: Seed through Series B/growth equity, with a preference for businesses with proven traction and product-market fit heading toward Series B. Known for exit-focused strategy (works with founders toward acquisition/scale outcomes).
- Check size: From ~$600K (≈R10M) at Series A up to $8M for expansion-stage rounds via Fund III.
- Caveat: Knife's stated sweet spot skews toward *proven traction/Series A* rather than true seed — Klar's pre-revenue soft-launch stage may be earlier than their typical entry point. Worth a warm intro for pipeline visibility even if not immediate fit.
- Application format: Direct approach via network/warm intro; no open online application form identified.
- Source: [Startup.Africa — Knife Capital](https://www.startup.africa/knife-capital-south-african-vc-backing-top-tech-startups/), [TechCrunch — Fund III close](https://techcrunch.com/2023/08/09/south-african-vc-knife-capital-closes-50m-series-b-fund-for-startups-with-high-exit-potential)

**Uprise.Africa** (equity crowdfunding platform, Johannesburg)
- Status: Active. South Africa's first regulated equity crowdfunding platform — the closest SA structural equivalent to the Seedrs campaign already drafted for the UK raise.
- Focus: General early-stage SA businesses raising equity from a broad investor base (retail + sophisticated investors), not fintech-specific.
- Check size / raise size: Platform-dependent; typical SA equity crowdfunding raises are in the low-to-mid single-digit millions of Rand. **[FOUNDER INPUT NEEDED: exact minimum/maximum raise size and platform fees should be confirmed directly with Uprise.Africa before drafting a campaign, as this research could not verify current published fee schedules.]**
- Regulatory note: Operates under Uprise Markets, an authorised Financial Services Provider (FSP) in South Africa — this is a regulated process similar in spirit to Seedrs' FCA-regulated status, but under SA's FSCA rather than the UK FCA. No SEIS/EIS-equivalent tax relief is attached (see Section 0.1).
- Application format: Online application/pitch submission through the platform; campaign built collaboratively with Uprise team, similar in shape to the Seedrs process already navigated for the UK raise.
- Source: [Uprise.Africa — SimplyBiz](https://www.simplybiz.co.za/uprise-africa), [Uprise.Africa official site](https://uprise.africa/)

### Tier 2: Fintech-specific fit, but gated by ownership/stage requirements

**AlphaCode Incubate** (Rand Merchant Investments / RMI-backed fintech incubator, Johannesburg)
- Status: Active, runs recurring annual cohorts; widely regarded as SA's most prestigious fintech-specific early-stage programme. R30M disbursed to 31 black-owned financial services businesses over the past five years.
- Focus: Fintech and financial services exclusively — payments, insurance, savings/investment, advisory, data analytics, blockchain. This is the single best sector-fit vehicle on this list.
- Structure/check size: Selection funnel from ~30 bootcamp entrants → 10 into a 12-week pre-incubation programme (R500K package, including R150K grant each) → 5 into full incubation with further investment access.
- **Hard eligibility gate: the applicant business must be at least 51% owned and managed by black South Africans.** This is a stated, non-negotiable criterion, not a scoring preference.
- **[FOUNDER INPUT NEEDED: this is the strongest fintech-sector fit on the list, but eligibility depends entirely on Klar's ownership/management composition, which this research does not have visibility into. Flagging for founder decision rather than assuming Klar does or doesn't qualify.]**
- Application format: Open call, online application form, published during active cohort windows.
- Source: [ITWeb — AlphaCode applications open](https://www.itweb.co.za/article/applications-for-alphacodes-incubate-programme-now-open/PmxVEMKlVXEqQY85), [Ventureburn — R16m call for black fintech startups](https://ventureburn.com/2019/05/alphacode-incubate-black-fintech-startups-apply-r16m/)

**National Empowerment Fund (NEF)**
- Status: Active government development finance institution.
- Focus: Broad sector coverage, explicit B-BBEE/transformation mandate. Funds range R250,000–R75 million via products including the iMbewu Fund (entrepreneurship finance) and Women Empowerment Fund.
- **Hard eligibility gate: minimum 51% black South African ownership required; must qualify as a black-owned Exempted Micro Enterprise (EME) or Qualifying Small Enterprise (QSE) under B-BBEE codes.** Preference given to applications demonstrating meaningful black women ownership.
- **[FOUNDER INPUT NEEDED: same ownership-composition dependency as AlphaCode above — cannot be assessed without founder input on current cap table.]**
- Application format: Formal application via NEF's own process (business plan, financials, funding criteria documentation) — heavier-weight than a VC pitch deck.
- Source: [NEF Funding Criteria](https://www.nefcorp.co.za/products-services/funding-criteria/), [SME South Africa — NEF Guide](https://smesouthafrica.co.za/sme-guides/everything-you-need-to-know-about-nef-funding/)

**SEFA (Small Enterprise Finance Agency)**
- Status: Active government lender. R50,000–R15 million, competitive rates (8–12% vs. 15–20% commercial bank rates), no collateral required for smaller amounts.
- Note: SEFA is a **loan** facility, not equity investment — different instrument type than the rest of this list, and would dilute nothing but adds debt service obligations.
- **Hard eligibility gate: requires CIPC (Companies and Intellectual Property Commission) registration and 100% South African ownership.** This almost certainly rules out SEFA for Klar as currently described (a UK-based product with SA users) unless a wholly SA-owned SA entity exists or is created specifically for this purpose.
- **[FOUNDER INPUT NEEDED: flagging as likely not viable given the 100% SA ownership requirement, unless the founder has structured or intends to structure a fully SA-owned local entity — this should not be assumed.]**
- Application format: Business plan, 6+ months bank statements, CIPC registration documents, ID; processed in 2–8 weeks via sefa.org.za or regional offices.
- Source: [ProTenders — SEFA loan guide](https://www.protenders.co.za/funding/guides/sefa-loan-application-complete-guide-south-africa), [SEFA official site](https://www.sefa.org.za/)

### Tier 3: Worth a look, lower confidence or narrower fit

**Technology Innovation Agency (TIA) — Seed Fund Programme**
- Status: Active (2026 call for Expressions of Interest from Implementing Partners was live at time of research), but structurally oriented toward **R&D commercialisation** — moving research outputs from Higher Education Institutions, Science Councils, and SMMEs into "prototypes and fundable ideas." This is a less natural fit for an already-shipped consumer SaaS product than for a pre-product research-stage innovation.
- Eligibility: Applicant must be a South African-registered SMME (or apply via a university/science council tech-transfer partner), tax-compliant, not insolvent/under debt review. Priority given to previously disadvantaged, youth, women, and disabled-owned SMMEs (a preference, not a stated hard gate, unlike NEF/AlphaCode — but still relevant to ownership composition).
- Check size/structure: Delivered through named "Implementing Partners" (incubators, tech-transfer offices, regional development agencies) rather than direct-to-startup; amounts vary by partner.
- **[FOUNDER INPUT NEEDED: TIA's R&D/prototype framing may not map well to Klar's stage (live, functional product) — worth a direct conversation with an Implementing Partner to see if it fits before investing drafting time here.]**
- Source: [Innovation Bridge — TIA Seed Fund EOI 2026](https://innovationbridge.info/ibportal/content/tia-seed-fund-programme-call-expressions-interest-implementing-partners-2026), [TIA Seed Fund Brochure](https://www.tia.org.za/core/uploads/2017/12/Seed-Fund-Brochure.pdf)

**Jozi Angels** (Johannesburg angel network)
- Status: Established angel network with 50+ startups in portfolio spanning Enterprise Applications, Consumer, HealthTech, and FinTech. No confirmed new investment activity was found specifically dated to 2026 in this research — worth direct outreach to confirm current activity level rather than assuming either an active or dormant status.
- Application format: Online pitch deck submission form on joziangels.co.za.
- Source: [Jozi Angels official site](https://joziangels.co.za/)

**Kalon Venture Partners** (Johannesburg)
- Status: Established fintech-focused VC (portfolio includes Ozow, Flow Com) but no confirmed new investment activity was found dated to 2026 in this research. Their branding still references "12J" from their original fund vehicle — verify directly whether this reflects an active, currently-investable fund structure or a legacy description, given 12J's 2026 sunset (see Section 0.1).
- **[Flag: do not assume Kalon is actively deploying capital in 2026 based on this research alone — confirm directly before investing significant outreach effort.]**
- Source: [Kalon Venture Partners official site](https://www.kalonvp.com/)

## 3. B-BBEE and transformation — summary for founder decision

B-BBEE (Broad-Based Black Economic Empowerment) compliance is **not a universal requirement** across all SA funding — generalist VCs (Knife Capital, 4Di Capital, HAVAÍC) and the equity crowdfunding platform (Uprise.Africa) do not appear to gate eligibility on ownership composition, based on this research. However, **two of the vehicles with the strongest strategic/sector fit for Klar — AlphaCode (fintech-specific) and NEF — have hard 51% black South African ownership requirements**, and SEFA requires 100% South African ownership generally.

Separately: companies with annual turnover of R10 million or less can obtain automatic B-BBEE Level 4 recognition without meeting scorecard targets (the "Exempted Micro Enterprise" exemption) — this is likely relevant to Klar's current pre-revenue/soft-launch size regardless of which funding vehicles are pursued, since B-BBEE status can matter for SA enterprise customers, procurement, or future institutional investors even outside the funding-application context.

2. Is pursuing B-BBEE-relevant structuring (e.g. bringing on black South African co-founders, investors, or equity partners to meet AlphaCode/NEF thresholds) something the founder wants to explore, or should SA fundraising focus exclusively on the ownership-agnostic Tier 1 vehicles (HAVAÍC, 4Di, Knife, Uprise.Africa)?
3. Does an SA-registered legal entity (CIPC-registered Pty Ltd) exist today, or would one need to be created to pursue any of these vehicles?
